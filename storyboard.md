# Title



Claim and Storyboard



# Claim Paragraph



AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini have all quickly become everyday tools for students all across every level of academics. They offers instant assistance with writing, problem solving, and their coursework. However, as younger generations gain easier and earlier access to these AI assistants, many students make a shift from using AI as a learning aid to needing to rely on it to complete all their work. This shift can be accidental where the student may not have realized it but isn't learning anything anymore throughout their coursework and instead is simply using AI to complete it for them. If left unchecked or if this becomes a habit at an earlier age, this early dependence greatly widens the gap between the students' completed work and their actual knowledge for the class. This will affect their ability to think critically, work independently, and to analyze information throughout their future. Therefore, even though these AI assistants offer simple short-term benefits, the future educational framework will have to regulate and keep the students' interactions with AI tools in control so that early reliance on AI doesn't hinder these students mental skills and independent learning.



# Storyboard



##### Section 1: The Psychological Drive for AI Dependence in Students



Reason: Students rely on AI assistants not only because it is useful, but also because it satisfies their psychological needs and builds trust.



Evidence:

* Cognitive behavioral models show how student trust turns into dependence.
* Research has found that there are psychological needs from AI-specific needs.
* There are studies that go on the same track with digital vulnerability and platform habits.



Key Terms: Cognitive trust, psychological need, digital vulnerability, platform reliance, dependence from habits



##### Section 2: The impact on Critical Thinking and Innovative Problem Solving



Reason: Over-reliance on AI tools during coursework creates a gap between the students completed work and their actual knowledge on the work, which weakens their critical thinking skills.



Evidence:

* Computational models have contrasted learning enhancement against learning dependency.
* There is data linking AI dependence with reduced creative and innovative thinking.



Key Terms: Learning enhancement versus dependency, innovative thinking, knowledge gap, independent problem solving



##### Section 3: Behavioral Patterns and Decision-Making in Collaboration between Humans and AI



Reason: The way individuals make decisions as well as how they perceive fairness or accuracy determines whether they will be able to use AI responsibly or passively.



Evidence:

* Studies on how individual decision-making styles drive the over-reliance on AI tools.
* Research shows how bias shapes user trust and reliance between humans and AI.



Key Terms: Decision-making patterns, perceived fairness, bias, humans and AI



##### Section 4: Preserving Human Knowledge in Future Educational Frameworks



Reason: To prevent future generations from losing the capacity of being able to complete work individually, educational systems must have to adapt to these changes and prioritize that the younger generations will continue to be educated properly.



Evidence:

* Analysis on why AI systems still depend on human judgement and oversight.
* Synthesis of modern educational models to propose boundaries for student AI use.



Key Terms: Active oversight, educational frameworks, authentic learning
